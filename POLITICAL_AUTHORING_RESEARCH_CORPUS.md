# Political authoring research corpus

Status: B14 production corpus. It governs the implemented Political Order model, its separation from represented institutions, and its coordinated use by society presets.

## Research frame

The replacement uses a small number of distinctions that have direct simulation value:

- North separates institutions, the constraints structuring interaction, from organizations that act within them. This prevents a council, guard force, or cooperative from being stored as though it were merely a rule or preference. [North, Institutions, Institutional Change and Economic Performance](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/institutions-institutional-change-and-economic-performance/AAE1E27DF8996E24C5DD07EB79BBA7EE)
- Crawford and Ostrom distinguish rules, norms, and shared strategies rather than treating every repeated political statement as one kind of fact. [Crawford and Ostrom, A Grammar of Institutions](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/grammar-of-institutions/7D37CD3BC5ED2D9FD57D2EE292958F47)
- Ostrom's IAD work separates position, boundary, authority/choice, aggregation, information, payoff, and scope rules. Those categories identify the missing dependencies behind CAO's current labels such as council, all residents, consensus, and protected. [Ostrom Workshop IAD framework](https://ostromworkshop.indiana.edu/courses-teaching/teaching-tools/iad-framework/index.html) and [Polski and Ostrom, An Institutional Framework for Policy Analysis and Design](https://ostromworkshop.indiana.edu/pdf/teaching/iad-for-policy-applications.pdf)
- Tsebelis models decision change through identifiable individual or collective veto players. This supports explicit bodies and consent requirements rather than a free-floating consensus option. [Tsebelis, Veto Players](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7rvv7)
- Lipsky demonstrates that implemented practice can diverge from written policy through the discretion and constraints of actual operators. CAO therefore stores policy, operator, and observed practice separately. [Lipsky, Street-Level Bureaucracy](https://www.russellsage.org/publications/book/street-level-bureaucracy)
- Procedural-justice research treats perceived fairness and institutional legitimacy as related but distinguishable from whether a person likes an outcome. CAO therefore derives legitimacy and support from represented procedures, outcomes, identities, and observations rather than storing them as synonyms for political belief. [Tyler and Rasinski, Procedural Justice, Institutional Legitimacy, and Acceptance](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/law-and-society-review/article/procedural-justice-institutional-legitimacy-and-the-acceptance-of-unpopular-us-supreme-court-decisions-a-reply-to-gibson/EE113BE3485AAE1B467410270F63DEAD)
- Comparative constitutional work treats founding design as consequential and historically bounded. That supports a compact landing arrangement for a new colony rather than asking the player to pre-author mature institutions. [Elkins, Ginsburg, and Melton, The Endurance of National Constitutions](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/the-endurance-of-national-constitutions/CD671879ADBFF7420C25C3707E6135F0)
- Comparative-democracy research compares interacting institutional dimensions rather than one universal government slider. CAO uses coherent assets and typed dependencies while keeping the player surface concise. [Lijphart, Patterns of Democracy](https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300172027/patterns-of-democracy/)

These works provide analytical distinctions, not a requirement that CAO advertise academic terminology to the player. Player-facing language remains direct RimWorld language.

## Society recipe boundary

A society preset is an authoring recipe over two existing owners. It applies one
complete Culture and one complete Political Order in the same action. Historical
recipes begin from a complete political base and replace the questions for which
the named society requires a materially different composition. The resulting
name and account are generated from those saved variables. Culture and Political
Order remain independently editable; represented institutions, Ideoligion,
founding terms, and event history are not written.

The numeric mixtures are transparent design priors, not fitted historical
measurements. Their historical scope and limitations remain documented with the
matching Culture preset in `CULTURE_RESEARCH_CORPUS.md`.

## Construct register

| ID | Precise CAO definition and owner | Literature anchor | Represented game evidence | Production consumers | Known limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| POL-NORM | A proposition a represented population holds about what political conduct, authority, procedure, distribution, or protection ought to be proper. Owned by a population/faction belief profile; never proof of current order. | Crawford/Ostrom rules versus norms; North institutions and beliefs about constraints | CAPoliticalBeliefs, population political-belief source, founding political beliefs | Pawn appraisal, political cognition, founding suggestion, belief/practice tension, act judgment | Current positions use the same keys as current order and usually lack scope, object, or condition. |
| POL-INSTITUTION | A durable represented arrangement of rules, positions, organizations, and expectations that structures repeated action. Owned by the faction or settlement whose interaction it governs. | North; Ostrom IAD | faction/settlement organization, relations, policies, customs, offices, programs | authorization, work, agreements, settlement authority, provision, security, generation | Current factionStructure is only a flat assertion and does not instantiate the arrangement. |
| POL-ORG | A durable collective actor with members, resources, records, and capacity to act. Owned by its organization record. | North's institution/organization distinction | CAOrganization, faction and settlement organizations, federations, households, worker or security groups | agreements, claims, obligations, policies, security, treasury, decision history | CAOrganization is broad; specific organization kinds need typed capabilities rather than generic customs. |
| POL-POSITION | A role that may be occupied and to which rights, duties, and authority attach. Owned by an institution or organization. | Ostrom position rules | CAOffice, Ideoligion roles, squad/team leads, household heads | command checks, succession, display, future body membership | CAOffice has holder, grants, seniority, and succession, but lacks jurisdiction, selection, term, removal, and accountable body. |
| POL-BOUNDARY | A rule determining who may enter, leave, or occupy a position or membership status. Owned by the relevant body or organization. | Ostrom boundary rules | faction affiliation, organization membership relations, pawn groups, kinship and residence facts | membership, franchise, service eligibility, admission | Current membership and participation options name categories without authority, criteria, review, or exit. |
| POL-AUTHORITY | A rule stating which actor or body may perform which action in which domain and conditions. Owned by the institution granting it. | Ostrom authority/choice rules | office grants, delegated responsibilities, settlement-authority relations, command checks | binding orders, agreements, work, security, policy changes | Current leadership labels do not define domains or limits; grants is free text. |
| POL-AGGREGATION | A rule determining how several participants' inputs become one binding decision. Owned by a decision body and scoped to a decision class. | Ostrom aggregation rules; Tsebelis veto players | founding founder vote, agreement acceptance, future council/assembly procedure | policy adoption, office selection, amendment, agreements | Current decree, majority, consensus, and custom options have no body, eligibility, quorum, threshold, tie, or veto record. |
| POL-INFORMATION | A rule controlling what must, may, or must not be known, disclosed, recorded, or communicated before action. Owned by an institution or procedure. | Ostrom information rules | knowledge records, warnings, decision history, communication and awareness systems | informed political judgment, notice, agreement process, accountability | No current political-axis option owns information procedure; most political decisions have no notice or disclosure contract. |
| POL-PAYOFF | A rule assigning material reward, burden, sanction, compensation, or standing consequence to an action or outcome. Owned by the rule or agreement imposing it. | Ostrom payoff rules | relation compensation, obligations, treasury, support change, sanctions, standing | work, taxation, protection, compliance, political response | Current work/status/support labels collapse obligation and consequence; sanctions are only partially represented. |
| POL-SCOPE | A rule defining allowed, required, or forbidden outcomes and the population, territory, assets, or decision class covered. Owned by the relevant rule. | Ostrom scope rules | relation scopes, agreement scopes, map/settlement/faction owners, asset/program keys | validation, jurisdiction, conflict rules, provision, property | Flat axes are predominantly unscoped, which turns ordinary multi-level coexistence into contradiction. |
| POL-POLICY | A presently adopted substantive rule or commitment directing conduct or allocation. Owned by the adopting organization and linked to authority, scope, and enforcement. | North; Crawford/Ostrom institutional statements | CAPolicyRecord, founding terms, agreements, provision and custody rules | schedules, custody, defense posture, construction, distribution, conflict conduct | Current policies are keyed values with uneven linkage to authorizing decisions and implementation. |
| POL-PRACTICE | A repeated, evidenced way actors actually implement or evade a rule. Owned by the acting organization/population and derived from longitudinal observation. | Crawford/Ostrom enduring regularities; Lipsky implementation discretion | CAOrganizationCustom with provenance, culture practices and observations, security practices, repeated decision history | cultural history, belief/practice conflict, public support, future generation | A generic custom label is presently used as a substitute for several missing institutions. |
| POL-EVENT | A dated represented act or decision that occurred once. Owned by the event ledger with actors, targets, context, and provenance. | Lipsky's attention to actual implementation; institutional-grammar action statements | CAActRecord, CADecisionEntry, relation changes, combat conduct, warning/response records | memories, political judgment, practice derivation, history | One event can evidence a practice or violate a norm but cannot alone prove a durable institution. |
| POL-LEGITIMACY | A population- and institution-specific appraisal that an authority, procedure, or action has a rightful claim to compliance. Derived from belief fit, procedural treatment, outcomes, identity, knowledge, and history. | Tyler and Rasinski; procedural-justice literature | CAInstitutionLegitimacyAppraisal, cultural cognition, known events and procedures | compliance, political attitudes, organization decisions, narration | Current support and legitimacy paths remain incomplete and some institutional inputs are only labels. |
| POL-SUPPORT | A contingent willingness to sustain, cooperate with, or accept an organization or office. Derived and changeable; not the same as legitimacy or agreement with one policy. | Tyler; political-support tradition | CAOrganization.publicSupport, conduct and agreement history, needs, affinity | agreement willingness, leadership binding capacity, UI | One scalar cannot explain constituencies or issue-specific support; retained only as an aggregate output with traceable appraisals. |
| POL-PERCEIVED | What a pawn or population believes others commonly do, approve, or expect. Owned by the perceiver's knowledge/cultural state and derived from observed evidence. | Crawford/Ostrom norms and shared strategies; IAD interactions/outcomes | CACultureQuestionDistribution descriptive norm, visibility, confidence, observations | social influence, behavior appraisal, political cognition | Perceived norm must not be copied into current law or actual practice without evidence. |
| POL-FOUNDING | The limited rules a newly arrived population chooses to apply immediately at landing. Owned by the founding colony; it is the first instituted state, not a mature constitution. | Elkins/Ginsburg/Melton on founding design and endurance | CAFoundingArrangement, applied relations and organization decisions | initial authority, work, decision voice, supplies, term, later tension | Current four booleans are appropriately bounded but need typed owners and dependency-safe presets. |
| POL-DELEGATION | A revocable or durable grant of named responsibilities from one represented authority to another. Owned by the relation recording grantor, recipient, scope, term, and origin. | Ostrom authority/scope; Tsebelis institutional actors | federation and settlement-membership relations, delegatedResponsibilities, origin and sunset | faction/settlement authority, federations, agreements, defense | Current federated leadership option duplicates and underspecifies this stronger substrate. |
| POL-PROPERTY | Rules and claims assigning rights to use, exclude, transfer, inherit, receive returns from, or govern a class of assets. Owned by claimants and governing institutions. | North on institutions/incentives; Ostrom rules-in-use | CAClaim, ownership/compensation relations, organization property customs, map things and facilities | taking judgments, work, production, trade, provision | Current ownership axis assigns one regime to all farms/workshops and lacks asset classes and rights bundles. |
| POL-LABOR | A represented work relation or obligation stating worker, assigning authority, task/domain, consent, duration, compensation, exit, exemption, and enforcement. Owned by the relation or institution. | Ostrom authority/payoff/boundary; Lipsky implementation | CARelation responsibilities/compensation, work orders, founding work rule, organization groups | assignment, compelled-work judgment, production, support | Current work options conflate employment, worker self-government, household allocation, and public duty. |
| POL-PROVISION | An institution or program that supplies named needs through an operator, funding or stock source, eligibility rule, service level, and failure behavior. Owned by an organization and settlement program. | Ostrom action arena and rules; public-administration implementation | CAProvisionArrangement, stores, facilities, operators, obligations, stocks | starting provisions, emergency support, settlement generation, runtime reconciliation | Current support/economy labels do not consume most of the existing provision substrate. |
| POL-SECURITY | A represented enforcement or defense organization with members, command, jurisdiction, powers, duties, equipment, funding, and accountability. Owned by its organization and mandate. | Ostrom positions/authority/scope; Lipsky operator discretion | CASecurityPractice, guard pawn IDs, defense assignments, offices, policies, facilities | custody, local order, defense, incident response, generation | Current localOrder and defense options name organizations without instantiating them; aperture generation reads labels directly. |
| POL-CONFLICT | A normative standard, adopted rule, and separate event history governing targets, surrender, custody, quarter, noncombatants, command, and sanctions in conflict. Each layer retains its own owner. | Crawford/Ostrom norm/rule distinction; IAD scope and payoff | political standards, CAActRecord violence circumstances, custody/protection relations | pawn memories, political response, conflict legitimacy, history | Current warConduct mixes broad permission and specific protections; only some events are observed and judged. |

## Modeling rules derived from the corpus

1. Belief never proves institution, policy, practice, or event.
2. An institution requires owners and linked rules; a label is insufficient.
3. A procedure requires a body, eligible participants, scope, and aggregation rule.
4. An organization requires represented members or operators before it can produce effects attributed to that organization.
5. Policies state adopted rules; practices and events establish what happened.
6. Legitimacy, support, and perceived norms are derived appraisals with evidence, not authoring shortcuts.
7. Multiple institutions may coexist when their scopes differ. Contradiction is evaluated at the shared owner, body, domain, population, asset class, and time.
8. Creator assets may populate several dependent facts, but every populated fact retains its own owner and provenance.
9. The player founding surface instantiates only immediate landing rules. Mature offices, bodies, security organizations, and administrative practice develop through play unless a scenario explicitly supplies them.
10. Research identifiers remain in documentation and code comments where useful; player-facing copy names the concrete game fact.
